## What the lab values

What the lab aims at is to work control theory out properly and put it on the record, as conference talks and papers. We do not stop at "it ran" or "the numbers came out": what matters is making clear what is new, and why the method works.

At the same time, laying out a route on which ordinary, steady effort produces a real result is the supervisor's job too. Plans here are not built on excessive workloads or unreasonable demands. Through the choice of topic, the reading, the meetings and the daily discussion, the aim is that you gradually become able to carry the research yourself.

In research, the answer is almost never known at the outset. You look things up for yourself, cut a large problem into pieces small enough to attack, think, try them, and explain what came out to other people. That way of approaching problems outlasts any particular topic you worked on.

Research is not easy, and it does not always go as planned. Even so — thinking about a hard problem, working through it by trial and error, and becoming able to do something you could not do before is where the interest lies. One goal is that nobody leaves this lab with regrets.

## Day-to-day

Students come to the lab on weekdays as a rule, because being able to see the supervisor, the senior students and the other members, and to talk about the research as a matter of course, matters too. There is no core time, though. If you need a weekday off, that is fine as long as you say so in advance.

Research reports and seminars are each held about once a week. A research report is not only for sharing progress: it is where we sort out what is giving trouble and what to take on next, and where the discussion goes into detail when it needs to. Students who have just started get fairly close support from the supervisor and from senior students.

You are not expected to sit on a problem alone for long. Thinking it through yourself matters, but when you are stuck, ask the supervisor or the other members — informally is fine.

Job hunting and other activities outside the research are largely left to your own judgement. If the research stalls for a while, making the time up when things ease off is enough.

## How research topics are chosen

Topics start from a broad direction suggested by the supervisor and take shape through discussion.

A fourth-year project begins with understanding control theory the lab has already built and checking it in simulation and on real hardware. Building a new control theory single-handedly is not asked for at that stage. What counts instead is understanding the theory you are working with well enough to explain it in your own words, picking up the related background, and getting an experiment working yourself.

As you get used to research, you take on a growing share of finding the weaknesses in existing methods and deciding what to look into and try next. The aim, in the end, is that you can carry the research on your own.

## Conferences and papers

Results are presented actively, at domestic and international conferences and as papers.

You do not need to know how to read or write a paper, or how to give a conference talk, when you start. Finding and reading the literature, putting results together, writing them up and presenting them are all taught here.

## Joining the lab

### Students at Nagoya Institute of Technology

Undergraduate lab assignment follows the department's rules, and entry to the graduate school is decided by the entrance examination. Neither is the lab's decision to make, so check the department's and graduate school's own announcements for the procedure and the dates.

### Students at overseas universities

If you are thinking of joining from outside Japan, email me with your CV, your transcript, and a short account of what you would like to work on. I will normally suggest an online meeting before anything else, so that we can both judge honestly whether the fit is right — including whether the topic you have in mind is one this lab can support well.

Admission itself goes through the university rather than the lab: the application routes and the entrance examination are set by Nagoya Institute of Technology, and the outcome is not mine to decide. What I can do is talk it through with you first, and point you at the right route once we know what you are aiming for.

## Visits and contact

If you have questions about the research or about how the lab is run, get in touch at any time, whether or not assignment is close. You are welcome to visit. The contact address is on the [People]({{ base_path }}/people/) page, and the location is on the [Access]({{ base_path }}/access/) page.

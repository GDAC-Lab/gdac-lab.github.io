## What the lab values

We are a control theory laboratory centred on the control of rotational motion. Building a theory is only part of the work: checking it in simulation and on real hardware is part of the same piece of research.

- **Theory and hardware, back and forth.** What is shown in equations is tested in a simulator and on real vehicles. When it does not work, finding out why is the point
- **Explaining your own work.** Seminars and conferences are practice in telling other people what you have done
- **Publishing what can be published.** The simulator developed in the lab is [public]({{ base_path }}/simulator/), and research is carried out on the assumption that it will be presented and published

### Graduate students

We accept students continuing from our undergraduate programme and students from other universities. Research topics are chosen in consultation, in line with the lab's [research themes]({{ base_path }}/research/) and the student's interests.

**In preparation:** entrance-examination dates and application procedures follow the university's guidance; points we would like applicants to confirm with us beforehand will be listed here.

### Students at overseas universities

We can host students through the exchange programmes run by Nagoya Institute of Technology; see the [Student Exchange Program](https://www.nitech.ac.jp/eng/admissions/programs/exchange.html) page. Applications are often due around five months before the intended start, so please contact us early.

## How research topics are chosen

Topics are chosen so that they relate to one of the pillars on the [Research]({{ base_path }}/research/) page: control of rotational motion, and wheeled drones for infrastructure inspection. Rather than fixing a topic on day one, new members work on fundamentals for a while and narrow down as their interests take shape.

## What you will learn and use

It depends on the topic, but most students meet the following.

- Control theory, from linear control to geometric control of rotational motion
- Simulation and analysis in MATLAB and Python
- Physics simulation with [MuJoCo](https://mujoco.org/), using the [simulator]({{ base_path }}/simulator/) the lab develops and publishes
- Experiments on real vehicles such as drones
- Managing code with Git and GitHub

## Conferences and papers

Results are meant to be presented at conferences in Japan and abroad and published as papers. Students present their own work (see the [news]({{ base_path }}/) on the home page), and the lab supports the preparation.

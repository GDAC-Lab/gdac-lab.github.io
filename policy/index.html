    Lab operating guidelines

    This page is provisional. It states only what has been decided and marks the rest as “in preparation”. Details of affiliation timing and procedures will be added as the university’s schedule is confirmed.

    What the lab values

    We are a control theory laboratory centred on the control of rotational motion. Building a theory is only part of the work: checking it in simulation and on real hardware is part of the same piece of research.

    • Theory and hardware, back and forth. What is shown in equations is tested in a simulator and on real vehicles. When it does not work, finding out why is the point
    • Explaining your own work. Seminars and conferences are practice in telling other people what you have done
    • Publishing what can be published. The simulator developed in the lab is public, and research is carried out on the assumption that it will be presented and published

    Joining the lab

    Undergraduate students

    So far, undergraduate students have joined the lab in October. Procedures and timing follow the department’s schedule; please check the department’s announcements for the official information.

    If you are considering the lab, you are welcome to visit or ask questions beforehand. Contact details are on the People page.

    Graduate students

    We accept students continuing from our undergraduate programme and students from other universities. Research topics are chosen in consultation, in line with the lab’s research themes and the student’s interests.

    In preparation: entrance-examination dates and application procedures follow the university’s guidance; points we would like applicants to confirm with us beforehand will be listed here.

    Students at overseas universities

    We can host students through the exchange programmes run by Nagoya Institute of Technology; see the Student Exchange Program page. Applications are often due around five months before the intended start, so please contact us early.

    How research topics are chosen

    Topics are chosen so that they relate to one of the pillars on the Research page: control of rotational motion, and wheeled drones for infrastructure inspection. Rather than fixing a topic on day one, new members work on fundamentals for a while and narrow down as their interests take shape.

    What you will learn and use

    It depends on the topic, but most students meet the following.

    • Control theory, from linear control to geometric control of rotational motion
    • Simulation and analysis in MATLAB and Python
    • Physics simulation with MuJoCo, using the simulator the lab develops and publishes
    • Experiments on real vehicles such as drones
    • Managing code with Git and GitHub

    Conferences and papers

    Results are meant to be presented at conferences in Japan and abroad and published as papers. Students present their own work (see the news on the home page), and the lab supports the preparation.

    Visits and contact

    Visitors are welcome — prospective undergraduate and graduate students, and people from companies and other universities alike. Please get in touch by e-mail beforehand; contact details are on the People page. The student room is Room 1010 on the 10th floor of Building 3; directions are on the Access page.
